# Reading old recipe cards

What the abbreviations, old words and old measures on family recipe cards mean, and how to write them for
From Scratch. The rule throughout: keep her word, make it readable, and explain it once in `notes` if a young cook
wouldn't know it.

## Abbreviations

| On the card | Means | Write |
|---|---|---|
| c., C., cup | cup | `cup` / `cups` |
| T., Tb., Tbs., Tbsp., tbl. | tablespoon (a capital T) | `tbsp` |
| t., tsp., teasp. | teaspoon (a small t) | `tsp` |
| oz. | ounce | `oz` |
| lb., lbs., # (as in "1# butter") | pound | `lb` |
| pt., qt., gal. | pint, quart, gallon | `pint`, `quart`, `gallon` |
| pkg., pkt., env. | package, packet, envelope | `package`, `packet`, `envelope` |
| sq. | square (of baking chocolate, 1 oz each) | `square` |
| doz. | dozen | `dozen` |
| lg., med., sm. | large, medium, small | spelled out |
| B.P., bk. pdr. | baking powder | `baking powder` |
| soda, B.S. | baking soda | `baking soda` |
| van. | vanilla (extract) | `vanilla` |
| cinn. | cinnamon | `cinnamon` |
| br. sugar | brown sugar | `brown sugar` |
| conf. sugar, 10X, XXXX | confectioners' (powdered) sugar | `confectioners' sugar` |
| w/, w/o, & | with, without, and | spelled out in steps |
| mod. | moderate (oven) | `moderate oven (350°F)` |

**Capital T or small t?** Compare the letter's height with the other capitals on the card. When you can't tell, think
about the amount: 1 T. of salt in a dozen cookies is unlikely, 1 t. of salt is usual. If it's still unclear, write your
best reading and put it in `unsure` ("could be 1 tsp or 1 tbsp of salt").

## Old ingredients

Keep the word on the card; add a line to `notes` when it helps.

| On the card | What it is |
|---|---|
| oleo, oleomargarine | margarine (butter works in almost every old recipe) |
| sweet milk | ordinary milk, as opposed to sour milk or buttermilk |
| sour milk | milk soured on purpose: 1 tbsp vinegar or lemon juice in a cup of milk, left 5 minutes. Buttermilk also works |
| top milk | the cream-rich top of a bottle of unhomogenised milk; half-and-half is close |
| sweet cream | cream |
| Crisco, shortening | solid vegetable shortening |
| Karo | corn syrup |
| Eagle Brand | sweetened condensed milk (not evaporated) |
| Pet milk, Carnation | evaporated milk (not sweetened condensed) |
| graham flour | whole wheat flour |
| nutmeats | shelled nuts |
| oatmeal (in baking) | usually rolled oats, uncooked |
| castor or caster sugar | superfine sugar |
| icing sugar | confectioners' sugar |
| Bisquick, Jell-O, Cool Whip, Velveeta, Durkee… | brand names: keep them as written |

## Old measures

These are "about" amounts. Write the card's words in the ingredient line when the recipe depends on them, and put
the modern amount in `notes`, not in place of hers.

| On the card | About |
|---|---|
| a scant cup | a little less than a cup: write `1 scant cup` |
| heaping, heaped, rounded | more than level: write `1 heaping tbsp` |
| butter the size of an egg | ¼ cup |
| butter the size of a walnut | 2 tbsp |
| a teacup | ¾ cup |
| a coffee cup | 1 cup |
| a wineglass | ¼ cup |
| a gill | ½ cup |
| a saltspoon | ¼ tsp |
| a dessertspoon | 2 tsp |
| a dash | ⅛ tsp or less |
| a pinch | about 1/16 tsp |
| a stick of butter or oleo | ½ cup (8 tbsp, 4 oz) |
| 1 square chocolate | 1 oz unsweetened baking chocolate |
| 1 pkg. yeast, 1 envelope gelatin | ¼ oz, about 2¼ tsp |
| 1 lb. box confectioners' sugar | about 3¾ cups |

## Cans and packages

Package sizes have changed over the years. Keep the size the card gives (`"1 (6 oz) pkg. chocolate chips"`). If the
card gives none, keep the package as written and say in `unsure` that today's size may differ: cake mixes shrank from
about 18¼ oz to 15¼ oz, chocolate chips come in 12 oz bags where a card may mean the old 6 oz (1 cup), and gelatin
comes in 3 oz and 6 oz boxes.

Old can numbers:

| Can | About |
|---|---|
| No. 1 | 10½ oz, 1¼ cups |
| No. 300 | 14–16 oz, 1¾ cups |
| No. 303 | 16–17 oz, 2 cups |
| No. 2 | 20 oz, 2½ cups |
| No. 2½ | 27–29 oz, 3½ cups |
| No. 10 | 6½–7 lb, 12–13 cups |

Write a can number the way From Scratch reads a size: `"1 No. 2 can crushed pineapple"` stays readable, but
`"1 (20 oz) can crushed pineapple (a No. 2 can)"` scales and converts. Prefer the second.

## Ovens

| On the card | Write |
|---|---|
| very slow oven | `very slow oven (250–275°F)` |
| slow oven | `slow oven (300–325°F)` |
| moderate oven | `moderate oven (350°F)` (moderate spans 350–375°F; 350°F suits most cakes and cookies) |
| moderately hot, quick oven | `quick oven (375–400°F)` |
| hot oven | `hot oven (400–450°F)` |
| very hot oven | `very hot oven (450–500°F)` |
| Regulo or gas mark 1 to 9 (British cards) | 1 = 275°F, 2 = 300°F, 3 = 325°F, 4 = 350°F, 5 = 375°F, 6 = 400°F, 7 = 425°F, 8 = 450°F, 9 = 475°F |
| 350° with no letter | 350°F (a card from a Fahrenheit country) |

If a range doesn't suit the recipe (popovers in a "moderate" oven), write what the card says and flag it in `unsure`.

## Old methods

Keep them as steps; they still work. "Cream the butter and sugar" (beat until light), "cut in" (work cold fat into flour
until crumbly), "scald the milk" (heat until just below boiling; not needed for pasteurised milk, but harmless),
"bake until it tests done" or "until a straw comes out clean" (a toothpick works). If the card gives no time, don't
invent one; flag it.

## Handwriting traps

- **1 and 7, 4 and 9, 3 and 8.** Check against the other numbers on the card, and whether the amount makes sense.
- **Fractions.** A faint bar turns ½ into 12 and ¼ into 14. "1 1/2" with the space lost reads as "11/2": it's 1½.
- **"oz" and "02", "lb" and "16".** Units come after numbers.
- **c and e, a and o, u and n.** Read the word, not the letter: "1 c. sugar", not "1 e. sugar".
- **Ditto marks** (" or do.) repeat the line above.
- **Recipes that run onto the back**, or onto a second card clipped behind. Ask for the back if a step seems to stop.
